How a vote is counted

Brazil votes on 25 October, the US on 3 November, Spain on 29 November. Here is how each one counts, from polling station to official result, with each electoral authority as the source.

No polls, no predictions, no picking sides. Five short videos in English go with this page; their voices are synthetic.

Try it: read the count at the source

Our experiment · 5 minutes, before the night

Done for Spain: the first number is provisional, from the Government; the official one is the Boards' count, published in the BOE.

  1. Open one official page. Brazil: the TSE's Resultados. US: your election office, via USA.gov. Spain: Infoelectoral. [5] [32] [40]
  2. Find two things. What the first number is called, and who gives the official one.
  3. Say them to one person. Close the page. That's it. You don't have to learn anything.

On your own? Write them on paper and leave it by the door.

When a result lands on my screen, I ask: is this number official yet?

Where this comes from

The guide is each country's electoral authority: the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) in Brazil; state and local election offices, with the federal Election Assistance Commission (EAC), in the United States; the BOE and the Junta Electoral Central in Spain. We opened every source on 16 October 2026. Each step of the count rests on the law and the authority that runs it. Where a question is about values, both sides speak in their own words, with their names. Corrections welcome.

Two hard questions

How long would you wait for certainty? An election official and the association of state election chiefs, in their own words:

For results sooner. Francisco Aguilar, Nevada Secretary of State, May 2024: "Voters deserve available results on election night; releasing results sooner will increase transparency [...]" [31]

For accuracy first. The National Association of Secretaries of State (NASS), August 2024: "Every election official's primary focus is accuracy to ensure every eligible vote counts." [20]

What would you need to know before trusting a first count? Two things to know about Brazil, side by side:

  • The TSE: "é possível conferir os resultados que compõem a totalização" (it is possible to check the results that make up the total; our translation). [2]
  • The Carter Center, 2022: the machine design "places the onus of results integrity on the voting software. The TSE has provided an extensive set of audit procedures to address this." [10]

Both questions stay open.

Each country, step by step

Brazil

  1. At the section. Each machine prints its bulletin, the boletim de urna, and one copy goes on the door.
  2. Off the internet. No network connection. A digital signature confirms each result came from the right machine.
  3. The first number. Results for president start at 17:00 Brasília on the TSE's Portal Resultados.
  4. Check it yourself. The free Boletim na Mão app reads a bulletin's QR code and compares it with the TSE's data.

The official date. Not in our sources yet. We add it when we read it.

Sources: [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

The TSE's calendar set any runoff for 25 October, and the TSE's official results show that nobody passed 50 percent of the valid votes for president on 4 October. [2] [9] Voting runs from 8:00 to 17:00 Brasília time, and anyone in line at 17:00 can vote. Voting hours shift by time zone so the whole country votes at the same moment: in Acre, 6:00 to 15:00 local time. [2] [3] [15] The machine prints five copies of its bulletin, with the votes for each candidate and the turnout. [2] [3] Results go to the electoral boards over a network used only for that, and the digital signature is checked before they are added up. [2] [6] [7] Results for president show for the whole country and for voters abroad at once, and update as bulletins come in. [3] [4] [5] The TSE posts the bulletins online and, at the end, on its open data portal. [3] [5] [12] To win the runoff, a candidate needs a majority of valid votes; blank and null votes are not counted as valid. [1] [8]

United States

  1. Mail ballots. Each state sets when they are processed. None releases those results before polls close, says NCSL.
  2. The first number. Unofficial on election night, even at "100% of precincts" or after a media call.
  3. The official count. The canvass confirms every valid ballot, mail and provisional ones included.
  4. The date. Each state sets its own: Michigan's county boards meet by 5 November, and Georgia's state deadline is 20 November.

Sources: [19] [23] [24] [25] [27]

Federal law sets the day; each state sets its polling hours. [17] [18] [32] [33] In NCSL's table, updated 15 September 2026, 44 states allow mail ballots to be processed before Election Day. [23] Only election officials provide official results. The canvass counts mail, military and overseas, early, Election Day and provisional ballots. [19] In Michigan, county canvass boards meet by 9:00 a.m. on Thursday 5 November. In Georgia, counties certify by 17:00 on Monday 9 November and the state by 20 November. [25] [26] [27] [28] In 2024, nearly all states had counted nearly all their votes within a day; about half a dozen took two to ten days. [22]

Spain

  1. At the table. Counted in public: the table's president reads each vote aloud and posts the result at the entrance.
  2. The first number. Provisional, and the Government gives it on the night.
  3. The official count. The Provincial Electoral Boards count from Friday 4 to Monday 7 December.
  4. The date. The official results are the ones published in the BOE.

Sources: [35] [39] [40]

Royal Decree 806/2026 called the vote. [34] [37] [38] Anyone inside the polling place or at its door at 20:00 can vote. [35] [39] The table's president takes the envelopes out one by one and reads each choice aloud. Disputes are settled by majority, and the result goes into a record, the acta, posted at the entrance. [35] [39] [40] The Government's provisional results come from copies of those records. The general count starts on the fifth day and must end by the eighth. The Boards can fix material, factual or arithmetic errors, and cannot annul a vote. [35] [39] [40]

Why some US states take longer

  • MIT's Election Data and Science Lab: "States tended to report more slowly when they had large numbers of mail ballots, restricted preprocessing before Election Day, or allowed timely postmarked ballots to arrive after Election Day." The finding is from 2020, and the Lab says 2024 was consistent with it. It is an association. [21] [22]
  • In 2024, by the Lab's measure, a majority of the national presidential vote was counted in about two hours. The data are from Edison Research, via CNN. [22]
  • In NCSL's table, 33 states, plus DC and Puerto Rico, allow ballots to be scanned before Election Day. Of the other 17, seven allow it only after polls close. "Processing" means different things in different states. [23]
  • On 29 June 2026, in Watson v. RNC, the Supreme Court ruled 5 to 4 that federal election-day laws do not stop a state from counting mail ballots postmarked by Election Day that arrive up to five business days later, as in Mississippi. The Court counts "roughly 30 States" that accept at least some late-arriving ballots. [29] [30]
  • One state's rule, as an example: in Michigan, results "must not be generated, printed, or reported before 8 p.m. on election day." [24]
  • House members elected on 3 November start their term at noon on 3 January 2027. [17]

Brazil: what observers and the 2022 review said

  • The Carter Center, 4 November 2022: "pure direct recording electronic machine design places the onus of results integrity on the voting software. The TSE has provided an extensive set of audit procedures to address this." The same mission called the access given to auditors "the most permissive to date", and said it could not give an overall assessment of the count. We do not know if that description fits the 2026 machines. [10]
  • Brazil's Ministry of Defense reviewed the 2022 election. Congresso em Foco reported that the review compared bulletins with TSE data in 442 and 501 machines and found "0% de inconsistência". The TSE's note on the same report says it "não apontou a existência de nenhuma fraude ou inconsistência" (did not point to any fraud or inconsistency). As the press quoted the report, it also says: "não é possível afirmar que o sistema eletrônico de votação está isento da influência de um eventual código malicioso" (it is not possible to state that the electronic voting system is free from the influence of a possible malicious code; our translation). We could not open the report itself. [11] [12] [13] [14]

Spain after the vote
  • Congress has 350 seats. Each province gets two to start, and the other 248 are shared out by population; Ceuta and Melilla elect one each. Lists are closed and blocked. Seats go out province by province under the D'Hondt rule, and only lists with at least 3 percent of the valid votes in that province take part. [35] [39] [41]
  • The absolute majority is 176. To be invested as head of government, a candidate needs it in the first vote. In a second vote, 48 hours later, a simple majority is enough. [36]
  • After the general count there is one day to file a claim and one day to resolve it, then an appeal to the Junta Electoral Central, and then the courts, up to the Supreme Court. [35]
  • This is a snap election. Under the Constitution, the President of the Government can propose dissolving the Cortes, after the Council of Ministers deliberates and under the President's sole responsibility. It cannot happen while a motion of censure is under way, nor again within a year, with one exception. The law sets the vote on the 54th day after the call: 6 October plus 54 days is 29 November. The Government stays on, acting, until a new one takes office. [34] [35] [36]
  • The campaign runs from 0:00 on Friday 13 November to midnight on Friday 27 November. On Saturday 28 November, campaigning is over and no electoral propaganda may run. [34] [35] [39]
  • The Constitution requires the new Congress to be called within 25 days of the vote, so by 24 December. The decree sets Wednesday 23 December, at 10:00. [34] [36] [43]
  • In 2023, the OSCE/ODIHR expert mission found that "some 94 per cent of precincts were tabulated by 11 p.m." Official results came broken down by municipality only, and the mission recommended publishing them by polling station. One source, from 2023. [42]

What this page leaves out

This page leaves out polls, predictions about who wins, and candidates' names. In Spain the law bans publishing polls in the five days before the vote, 24 to 28 November. [35]

If election night gets loud

One question is enough: is this number official yet? If not, close the live count; the official one keeps its date. If it is more than tired, talk to someone now: the help lines are at the bottom of the page, and in Spain the line is 024. [44]
