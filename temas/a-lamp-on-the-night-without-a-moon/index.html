A lamp on the night without a moon

Why light a lamp on the darkest night?

A tale we made up, on a night that is real. And one thing to try before you sleep.

1 pieza en español sobre este tema, en la página en español

The tale

Our tale · The night is real [1] [20]

This tale is ours. It has cousins in two traditions: the lamps Ayodhya lit for Rama, as many Hindus tell it, and the ones Jains light for Mahavira.

The night is real. On Sunday 8 November, the main night of Diwali, the moon sets before the sun. That night there is no moon in the sky.

In our tale, a grandmother filled small clay lamps with oil and set them in a row by the door.

Her grandson counted them. Eleven.

"Why light lamps on the darkest night?" he asked.

"When else would you light them?"

Across the lane, one window stayed dark. An old man lived there by himself.

The boy looked at the window, then at the row, and pointed at one lamp.

"Take it," she said. "Small steps. Mind the flame."

He crossed the lane with the lamp in both hands and knocked. The old man opened the door. The boy held out the lamp, and the old man set it on his windowsill.

When the boy came home, the row by the door had a gap in it. His grandmother left the gap as it was.

From the lane, two houses were lit.

What the old man did with that lamp, we'll tell you soon.

Before you sleep

Our experiment · → Study[22] [24]

The grandmother's answer: when it's dark, one small light. Any night; on Diwali, 8 November.

  1. Sit on the bed and turn off the last light. One minute in the dark.
  2. Light one small light, warm and dim. A diya or a candle goes in a holder, on something steady, away from the bed. Watch it for a few breaths.
  3. Put it out, and sleep in the dark. Make sure a flame is out before you sleep or leave the room.

"When I turn off the last light, I sit one minute, then light one small light."

→ The three steps, in motion

That's it. You don't have to learn anything.

If you're on your own that night

Whether or not you keep Diwali, the steps stay the same. Before step 3, send one person a message: a word, or a photo of your light. In the days around Diwali many people visit and share sweets; if no one is near, your message is the visit. [3] [7]

The map

Where this comes from

The tale is ours. The night and its lamps belong to Diwali, kept by Hindus and, in their own way, by Jains. On the same night Sikhs mark Bandi Chhor Divas. For dates and rites the guide is Drik Panchang, a Hindu calendar of reference. BAPS Swaminarayan Sanstha and the Hindu American Foundation (HAF) speak from inside, with ISKCON for the vaishnava month of Kartik and the Ramakrishna Math for Bengal. For the Jain Diwali, a Jain federation (JAINA) and a Jain scholar. For Bandi Chhor Divas, SikhNet speaks from inside; the 2026 date comes from India's Ministry of Tourism, and no Sikh source from inside that we found gives it. If you keep it, tell us. We show no image of any deity and quote no sacred text.

Study (about the behavior)

  • Light and sleep researchers recommend a bedroom as dark as possible: a 2022 consensus says so. The NHS says sleep may come easier in a room that is "quiet, dark and cool". [22] [23]
  • Ordinary room light in the evening delays the body's melatonin, a signal of the body clock. In a lab study of 116 people, room light before bed delayed melatonin onset in 99% of them and cut its nightly duration by about 90 minutes. The 2022 consensus suggests dim light for the last three hours before bed. [24] [22]
  • If you keep a light on before bed, a warm, dim one is gentler on the body clock than a bright, bluish one. Common warm-white home LEDs (2,700 to 3,000 K) can meet the consensus evening target at about 30 lux. In one study, reading on a light-emitting e-reader before bed delayed the body clock and melatonin more than a printed book. [22] [25]
  • Light left on during sleep is linked to worse sleep in large groups of people followed for years. In one lab study, the 10 adults who slept one night at 100 lux had higher insulin resistance the next morning, though they did not report sleeping worse. These are associations and one small study. [22] [26]
  • In one minute the eyes only begin to adjust to the dark. Full adjustment takes tens of minutes, half an hour or more. [27]
  • The London Fire Brigade names diya lamps: never leave them unattended, keep them in a heat-resistant holder on a stable surface away from anything that can catch fire, and put them out when you leave the room, above all before bed. The NFPA says the same of candles, and that more than a third of home candle fires start in the bedroom. [28] [29]
  • On sleep in general: in a large group of people in the UK followed for years, regular sleep times are linked to lower mortality. [30]

Our experiment

One minute of dark before sleep, then one small light, then dark again: no study has tested it. It is ours, for this night, and belongs to no rite; a puja lamp is kept the way each family keeps it. The studies above speak to the parts: a small, warm light in the evening, and a dark room for sleep.

The small light of step 2 is the part with the least support. The studies used electric light, never a flame, and the consensus asks for dim light before bed and dark for sleep. That is why there is one light, and it goes out.

The night

In 2026 the main night of Diwali, Lakshmi Puja, is Sunday 8 November: in India, and also in New York and Medellín. The Government of India lists Diwali on that Sunday, and India's National Stock Exchange lists it as a holiday. [1] [15] [14]

Lakshmi Puja is done after sunset, in the window called pradosh, on the evening when the new-moon lunar day (amavasya) is still running. Drik Panchang bases the rule on the manuals Dharma Sindhu, Nirnaya Sindhu and Vratraj. [2] [1]

The amavasya runs from 11:27 on 8 November to 12:31 on 9 November in Delhi. In New York and Medellín it runs from 00:57 on 8 November to 02:01 on 9 November, local time. [1]

On 8 November the moon sets before the sun in all three cities; the chart below gives the times. The new moon is exact at 07:02 UTC on 9 November: 02:02 in New York and Medellín, 12:32 in Delhi. [20] [21]

The days of Diwali in 2026, about five: Dhanteras on Friday 6 November, Kali Chaudas on Saturday 7, Lakshmi Puja on Sunday 8, Govardhan Puja and the Gujarati New Year on Tuesday 10, Bhai Dooj on Wednesday 11. In Gujarat the season closes with Labh Pancham, on Saturday 14 November. [2] [4] [14]

The word and the lamp

Deepavali comes from Sanskrit: dipa, a lamp, and avali, a row. A row of lamps. [19] [8] [17]

A diya is a small clay lamp that burns oil. At Diwali they go in rows at doors and windows and outside the house. Today electric lights and candles shine too. [17] [8] [3]

The lamps go up on the darkest night of the month, the moonless one, as BAPS puts it. Hindus say they stand for light over darkness, knowledge over ignorance and good over evil. [7] [8] [17]

The stories

Diwali has more than one story. BAPS counts five: Krishna's victory over Narakasura, Prithu, Lakshmi rising from the churned ocean, the Pandavas' return from exile and Rama's return to Ayodhya. HAF and the BBC note that the stories change from region to region. Held by the tradition. [7] [8] [17]

Many Hindus tell that when Rama came home to Ayodhya after fourteen years of exile, the people lit lamps along his way. The BBC tells it as a dark night without a moon, with little lamps outside the houses so the new king and queen could find their way. [8] [17] [7]

On Diwali night Hindus worship Lakshmi, goddess of wealth and good fortune, together with Ganesha. [2] [8] [17]

In Bengal, Odisha and Assam the same new-moon night belongs to the goddess Kali: Kali Puja, on Sunday 8 November 2026 in Kolkata. The Ramakrishna Math lists it for that Sunday. [5] [10] [17]

Jains keep their own Diwali: the night Mahavira reached nirvana at Pavapuri, and the Jain year starts with it. [11] [12]

Sikhs mark the same day as Bandi Chhor Divas: the day Guru Hargobind, the sixth Guru, was freed from Gwalior Fort with 52 prisoners. [13] [16] [18]

In the month of Kartik, around October and November, vaishnava devotees, ISKCON among them, offer a small lamp to Krishna as Damodara every evening. [9]

The house and the door

Before Diwali, homes are cleaned and often painted again, and someone in the house draws a rangoli of colored powder at the entrance. [3] [7] [17]

The BBC writes that many Hindus leave doors and windows open so Lakshmi can come in. BAPS describes welcoming her at the entrance. [17] [7]

In the days around Diwali people visit relatives and friends and share sweets. BAPS says they do it to renew their bonds and to "sweeten" relations. [3] [7] [17]

As BAPS puts it, Diwali is also a time to forgive the past year's wrongs and to mend quarrels in the family. [7]

The new books

For many Hindu merchants, Gujarati families above all, Diwali is the day to bless new account books (Chopda or Sharda Pujan). The business year starts there. [4] [7] [17]

In Gujarat the new year begins the day after Diwali's new moon, on 10 November 2026, and many shops reopen with their new books on Labh Pancham, 14 November. [4] [7] [17] [8]

Indian stock exchanges close for Diwali but open for one symbolic hour, the Muhurat session, on the evening of Lakshmi Puja. NSE has set it for Sunday 8 November 2026; the time will be announced by circular. We tell it as a custom. Descriptive, not financial advice. [14] [3]

Where they disagree

Ashvin or Kartik. The same night has two month names. In the west, the south and Bengal the month is called Ashvin: BAPS writes "Aso vad 15 (Amaas), the final day of the Hindu year", and the Ramakrishna Math "Ashwina Amavasya". In the north it is Kartik, and the new moon falls in the middle of the month; the BBC writes "the night of the new moon in the month of Kartika". [6] [7] [10] [17]

Lakshmi or Kali. Drik Panchang: "While most people in India worship Goddess Lakshmi on Amavasya Tithi during Diwali, folks in West Bengal, Orissa and Assam worship Goddess Kali on new moon day." The two follow different hours: Kali Puja takes the day when the amavasya holds at midnight, Lakshmi Puja the day when it holds at pradosh. In 2026 both fall on Sunday 8 November. [5] [2]

Which story. BAPS: "There are five stories celebrating this day." HAF: "Many Hindus recognize Diwali as a day celebrating the return of Prince Rama", and "Other Hindus commemorate Diwali as the day Lord Krishna defeated the demon king Narakasura". The BBC: "the actual legends that go with the festival are different in different parts of India". [7] [8] [17]

The lamps and the Ramayana. HAF: "The residents of Ayodhya were overjoyed at the return of their rightful King and lit lamps in his honor." Writing from the Jain tradition, Yashwant Malaiya holds that "Diwali is not mentioned in Valmiki's Ramayana" or in Tulsidas's Ram-charit-manas. We write "many Hindus tell" and never "the Ramayana says", and we take no side. [8] [11]

The Jain Diwali. Malaiya: "on this occasion we celebrate the Nirvana of Lord Mahavira". JAINA: "It is celebration of NIRVANA KALYANAK and not enjoyment of worldly pleasures." On the Jain new year the two differ by a day: JAINA says it "commences with pratipada, following Diwali"; Malaiya, that it "will begin on Diwali". [11] [12]

How many days. Drik Panchang counts five, from Dhanteras to Bhaiya Dooj, with more in Maharashtra and Gujarat. HAF: "Generally, Diwali is celebrated over five days", and its toolkit speaks of "all six days". BAPS: "a joyful series of 8 festivals". We say about five. [2] [8] [7]
