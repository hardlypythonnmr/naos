## 1 · The tale {#the-tale}

Tradition · Held by the tradition · Sources [1] [2] [4]

I had this one from the church calendar.

The first of November is for the saints. Vatican News calls them the friends of God.

And the second? Around the year 1000, an abbot named Odilo, at the monastery of Cluny, gave it to all the faithful departed, and from there it spread.

Why does the day for the faithful departed come right after the saints? None of the sources we read says. That part's yours to wonder about.

Not every church keeps them in November. More on that below.

## 2 · One evening {#one-evening}

Sunday 1 or Monday 2 November, or any night. Ten minutes.

1. **Say one name out loud.** Someone you miss. Once, at the table or by your window. If out loud is too much, say it softly, or skip this step.
   Tradition: naming the dead, in Jewish prayer and in some churches. [15] [8] [4]
2. **Set one more place.** A plate, a cup, a chair. Leave it there through the meal.
   Our experiment: we found no sourced custom.
3. **Call someone older who's still here.** Tell them whose name you said, or ask how they are.
   Study: a program of friendly calls, not a family call. [23] [24]
4. **Next week, call the same person again.**
   Our experiment.

That's it. In one line: say one name, then call someone who's still here.

## 4 · Where this comes from {#where-this-comes-from}

(Folded. Opens on tap.)

### The two days

All Saints' Day is 1 November in the Catholic and Anglican churches, and in many Lutheran ones. In 2026 it falls on a Sunday. All Souls' Day, the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed, is 2 November, a Monday this year. The Church of England's calendar names both days. [1] [2] [4] [5] [9]

All Saints began with the martyrs. In Rome, on 13 May of 609 or 610, the Pantheon was dedicated to Mary and the martyrs. By the 800s, Rome kept the feast of all the saints on 1 November. [1] [4]

The second day goes back to Odilo, abbot of Cluny, around the year 1000. He made it the rule in the monasteries under him; the church of Liège, under its bishop Notger, took it up, and from there it spread. [2] [4]

In England the days around All Saints were called Allhallowtide. It is often counted as three days, 31 October to 2 November. [29]

Many keep the names of their dead. The early Church entered them in diptychs; at an Anglican cathedral in Kuching, people were invited to write them down for the All Souls' service in 2024, to be shown on screens; a Missouri Synod congregation reads the year's names on All Saints. [4] [8] [9]

On 2 November a Catholic priest may say three Masses. In August 1915, during the First World War, Benedict XV extended this to every priest of the Latin Church, after bishops in countries at war asked for it. Spain and Portugal already had it, and so did Latin America. [10] [11] [4]

### Where they disagree {#where-they-disagree}

**Praying for the dead, among the churches.** Each side in its own terms, in our paraphrase. We don't say who is right.

- Catholic. The Catechism teaches that those who die in God's grace, but still in need of purification, are purified after death; the Church calls this purgatory. From the start, it says, the Church has offered prayers for the dead, above all the Eucharist. [3] [4]
- Anglican. The Church of England's Articles of 1571 reject the Roman teaching on purgatory. A Westminster Abbey preacher said in 2016 that after the Reformation the Church made no provision for All Souls or for prayer for the dead, that a prayer book of 1928 allowed that prayer again, and that for most in the Church of England it has become normal. Its calendar keeps 2 November today. [6] [7] [5]
- Lutheran. Their own Apology of the Augsburg Confession (1531), article XXIV, says it does not forbid prayer for the dead, and rejects offering the Mass to free the souls of the dead. Lutherans read that passage in two ways. Confessional Lutherans, in the words of 1517.org, remember with thanksgiving on All Saints those who died in the Lord, and reject prayer for the fate of the dead tied to purgatory. In a Missouri Synod congregation, the names of those who died that year are read on All Saints. We read no source from the ELCA or other Lutheran churches. [9]
- Orthodox. Metropolitan Tikhon of the Orthodox Church in America writes that the dead can no longer help themselves, and that the living can help them through prayer. The source we read says nothing about purgatory, so neither do we. [12]

**In Jewish tradition**, outside that debate, the dead are prayed for in their own way. In Yizkor, the memorial prayer, the person is named, and God is asked to remember their soul. To remember, here, carries the sense of acting for their good. [15]

**When.**

- Western churches: fixed dates, the first and second of November. [1] [2]
- Orthodox: All Saints on the first Sunday after Pentecost (7 June in 2026), and the dead on several Saturdays through the year. In the Russian tradition, Demetrius Saturday fell on 24 October 2026 on the new calendar, as in the Orthodox Church in America. On the old one, as in Moscow, it falls on 7 November. [12] [13] [14]
- Jewish: each person has a day of their own, the yahrzeit, the anniversary of the death on the Hebrew calendar. A candle burns about a full day, and the mourner's prayer, the Kaddish, is said with the congregation. In many communities, Yizkor comes four times a year. [15] [16]
- Hindu: Pitru Paksha, a fortnight of the waning moon. In India in 2026 it ran from 26 or 27 September, depending on whether the full-moon day counts, to 10 October. [18]
- Chinese calendar, as Hong Kong keeps it: days for the ancestors. Ching Ming, when families honor their ancestors, often at the graves, fell on 5 April 2026. In Hong Kong's Chiu Chow community, the Yu Lan festival runs through the seventh lunar month, from 13 August to 10 September in 2026. In the words of Hong Kong's Intangible Cultural Heritage Office, its Buddhist or Taoist rites include paying homage to ancestors and pacifying wandering ghosts with clothing and food. Lingyin Temple, a Buddhist temple in Hangzhou, keeps its central day on the 15th of that month, which fell on 27 August. [19] [20]
- Philippines: families keep Undas on 1 and 2 November with visits to the graves of loved ones. In 2026 both are special non-working days. [21]
- Colombia: the All Saints holiday moves to Monday 2 November, the same day as All Souls, which isn't a holiday on its own. [22]

Chart: Only the Western churches keep their dead on 1 and 2 November. (Table version under the chart.)

**Why 1 November.** The Catholic Encyclopedia says Gregory III dedicated a chapel in Rome to all the saints and set its feast on 1 November, in the 700s, and Gregory IV extended the feast to the whole Church. Vatican News says England and Ireland kept 1 November from the 700s, and Rome took it up in the 800s. Both agree that by the 800s Rome kept it on that day. [4] [1]

**Odilo's year.** Vatican News gives 998. The Catholic Encyclopedia gives no year. We say around the year 1000. [2] [4]

### Study (about the behavior)

- 240 older adults who got meals at home received short, friendly calls from trained young volunteers for four weeks. On one of two loneliness measures, they felt less lonely than people who got no calls. One trial, in 2020, of a call program, not of calls between family. [23]
- In preregistered experiments, people underestimated how much others appreciate being reached out to. In another set, a voice call built a stronger bond than text, with no more awkwardness, and people expected less of it. [24]
- The WHO says about 1 in 6 people worldwide are affected by loneliness, and links it to worse health. [25]
- A review of ten studies in East and Southeast Asia found that rituals after a loss help people adapt. [26]
- Keeping a bond with someone who died has mixed results in studies: sometimes it helps, sometimes it doesn't. [28]
- For most people, grief eases with time and usually doesn't need treatment. [27]

### Our experiment

The extra place at the table, the name said before the meal, the second call a week later: no study tests these. We are trying them. We found no November custom with a source for the extra place, so we don't call it one. If it helps, tie the call to the meal: when I finish eating, I call.

### How we checked this page

On 16 October 2026 we opened the sources above one by one and checked each study for retractions (none). For each tradition, a source from inside it guides the reading: Vatican News and the Catechism for the Catholic days, the Church of England's own calendar and Articles, the Lutheran Apology of the Augsburg Confession, the Orthodox Church in America, My Jewish Learning, Drik Panchang, and the Hong Kong Observatory with a Buddhist temple's own pages for the Chinese calendar. We crossed each claim about a tradition with at least three sources, at least one from inside it. What we could not cross that way is listed below, not above. Where they disagree, we say so above. This page quotes no sacred text, and the church documents are in our paraphrase. Titles are as each page gives them; where a study sits behind a paywall, its source says so.

### What we could not confirm

- Chung Yeung (Sunday 18 October 2026; in Hong Kong the holiday moves to Monday 19) as a day for the ancestors at the graves: Hong Kong's heritage list says so, but we found no source from inside the tradition, Buddhist, Taoist or a clan's own. So it stays off the list above and the chart. [19] [20]
- Ching Ming and Yu Lan: the inside source we read is a Buddhist temple in Hangzhou, not one in Hong Kong. Taoist and clan sources did not turn up.
- The Lutheran position: we read the Apology, 1517.org and one Missouri Synod pastor. Other Lutheran churches may say it differently.
- Colombia's holiday: two sources, Radio Nacional and Noticias Caracol; the law itself did not open. How these days are kept in each region of Colombia: we found one source, so we don't tell it.
- The start of Pitru Paksha 2026: Drik Panchang and two newspapers; we found no government calendar of India.
- That church bells rang for the dead on the night of All Souls: one Catholic magazine says bells were rung at Allhallowtide in England until Elizabeth I stopped it [29]; we found no second source or historian we could read, so it stays off this page.
- Why 1 November: the two accounts above differ, and the date of Gregory III's chapel is itself debated.
- Whether All Saints was set on top of Samhain: some say so, historians dispute it, and we have not read the books. It stays off this page.
- That names are read at Catholic Masses in November: we found no Catholic source, so we used others.
- Chabad's pages on the yahrzeit did not open for us. My Jewish Learning guides that part for now.
- How many months make grief "prolonged": the American Psychiatric Association says a year for adults; the WHO's classification did not open.
- Official texts we could not open: Colombia's Law 51 of 1983, the Official Gazette of the Philippines, the 1915 decree in the Vatican's acts, India's Tele-MANAS site, the Philippine Department of Health, Mexico's Línea de la Vida page.
